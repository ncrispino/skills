The problem Two to four plain sentences. What is broken or missing, and for whom, with a concrete example.

Hypothesis What we expect to be true, stated so an experiment can show it wrong.

First experiment The simplest setup that tests the hypothesis in week 1 or 2.

We succeed if The result that confirms it: what is measured, compared with what, by how much.

We stop if The result that would make us stop or change direction.

1. Motivation

Why this matters, in one or two short paragraphs. Lead with a concrete example of the problem, not a general claim. Say who cares and what they would do with the result.

2. Challenges

What makes this hard. One bullet per challenge, each with why current methods do not already handle it. Each challenge should map to a design choice or an experiment below.

  • Challenge. Why it is hard, and what it implies for the design.
Could a simple baseline already solve this?Name the strongest cheap baseline (prompting, a linear probe, a heuristic, a bigger model, more data, an off-the-shelf tool). If we cannot say why it falls short, testing it is the first experiment.

3. Threat model

Safety and security projects only; delete this section otherwise. Be explicit about what is in and out of scope so the experiments test the right attacker.

AssetWhat we protect (data, model behavior, user, system).
AdversaryWho attacks: role, resources, motivation.
CapabilitiesWhat they can do: access (black or white box), inputs they control, number of queries, adaptivity.
GoalWhat counts as a successful attack, stated as something we can measure.
Trust boundaryWhat we assume is trusted (system prompt, tool outputs, the monitor).
Out of scopeAttacks we deliberately do not defend against, and why.

4. Approach

Start simple and climb only when the previous rung is measured. Each rung says what it is, what it costs, and what result unlocks the next one. The big idea is the top rung, not the first step.

RungWhatCostGate to next rung
R0Trivial baseline.The metric separates R0 from an oracle.
R1Strongest simple baseline.Headroom exists: R1 is far enough from the ceiling to matter.
R2Minimal version of our idea.R2 beats R1 by more than seed noise.
R3Full idea.
